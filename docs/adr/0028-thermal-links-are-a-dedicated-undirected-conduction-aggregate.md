# Thermal links are a dedicated undirected conduction aggregate, integrated in closed form and mass-free

Status: accepted (#793, #794–#798). Endpoint scope and duplicate-pair authoring policy amended by [ADR-0032](0032-thermal-links-join-typed-thermal-nodes.md) (#853); the conduction invariants below remain in force.

A compartment could move heat to exactly one place: the heat-only exchanger on its own `reservoirId`. Everything else about its thermal boundary was sealed, so a shared bulkhead could not conduct, a greenhouse could not warm its neighbour through a common wall, and a cold surface could not draw moisture out of an adjacent warm compartment. ADR-0026 had made the Atmosphere pathway thermally neutral on purpose — the pathway's closed form holds temperature fixed across the step, and heat crossing the same opening would break that constant-coefficient premise — which left the hole deliberate but unfilled. Filling it required six decisions that are not visible in the conduction code and that a later contributor would otherwise be tempted to reverse.

**Heat travels through its own undirected aggregate, independent of Atmosphere pathways.** `StationSystemsSettings::thermalLinks` holds undirected entries of `id`, `aAtmosphereId`, `bAtmosphereId` and `conductanceKWPerK`, with a declared `MaxStationThermalLinks` ceiling in the spirit of `MaxStationPathways`. It is deliberately not merged with `atmospherePathways` and shares no identity with it: a solid wall with no opening and an open hatch through a thin bulkhead are both physically real, and coupling the two identities would force an author to fake one in order to obtain the other. Nor is a link a Station connection: `StationConnectionProperties` carries `maximumThroughput` and `efficiency`, neither of which means anything for conduction, and wiring them in later would let energy vanish through a field never meant to be read there. The directed connection key is avoided outright because it would allow `A→B` and `B→A` to be authored as two entries and resolve the same wall twice per tick. `A` and `B` exist only to fix the sign of published flux, exactly as they do for pathways. The independence is tested rather than asserted: `thermalLinkPathwayIndependence` wires both aggregates between the same pair with counter-flowing compositions and shows each side's gas delta equals the pathway's published transfer with no link term, and each side's conducted total equals the link's own ledger with no pathway term.

**The conduction is linear and integrates in closed form per link.** For endpoint capacities `C_a`, `C_b` — the authored effective air-plus-structure `thermalCapacityKJPerK` — and conductance `G`:

```
dT_a/dt = -(G / C_a) * (T_a - T_b)
dT_b/dt = +(G / C_b) * (T_a - T_b)

k = 1 - exp(-G * (1/C_a + 1/C_b) * dt)
Q = C_a * C_b / (C_a + C_b) * (T_a - T_b) * k
```

`Q` is the heat moved `A→B` over the tick and `C_a·C_b/(C_a + C_b)` is the reduced heat capacity, the exact analogue of the pathway's volume-weighted terms. At `k = 1` both compartments land on the energy-weighted average `(C_a·T_a + C_b·T_b)/(C_a + C_b)`; at `k = 0` nothing moves. The equilibrium transfer is evaluated first, independently of `k`, and `expm1` is used rather than `1 - exp`, so even an enormous conductance tends to equilibrium instead of to an indeterminate form — the same guard the pathway diffusion pass carries. This is why heat could ride its own aggregate where it could not ride the pathway: two-body conduction is linear with temperature-independent coefficients in the first place, so nothing has to be held fixed for the exact solution to stay exact. Celsius is valid at the definition site because conduction depends only on the temperature *difference*, which is identical in Celsius and Kelvin; that is the opposite of the radiative path, which needs absolute temperature, and the comment sits on `ThermalLinkSettings` so the next contributor cannot apply the wrong rule to the wrong law.

**The existing passive star solve was not reused, on a hard technical ground rather than a stylistic one.** This is the most tempting wrong answer, because every other compartment-to-reservoir thermal exchange in the model already goes through `solvePassiveExchange`. That solver computes a coupled hub temperature as a capacity-weighted average, `T = (C_hub·T_hub + Σ w_i·T_i) / (C_hub + Σ w_i)`, and the expression is only sound because the hub's own heat capacity is strictly positive — the reservoir always contributes capacity, and with it energy, to the average it settles on. A wall is a zero-capacity connector: it stores nothing and holds no temperature of its own, so setting `C_hub = 0` both degenerates the denominator when no leaf is live and strips the hub out of the weighting, leaving a conductance-weighted average that is not the energy-weighted average the two-body law requires. Independently, a thermal reservoir carries semantics a wall must not acquire — declared service temperature limits, radiator rejection, and a whole set of absorbed / routed / condenser ledger terms — and making compartments and reservoirs the same kind to reuse the solver would import all of them. The closed form is also chosen over explicit Euler for robustness rather than for stability at sane values: the reference station's 40,000 kJ/K compartment against a 1 kW/K wall relaxes with a time constant of hours against a 0.1 s step, where Euler is perfectly stable, but a 100,000 kW/K conductance against a 1 kJ/K compartment is authorable and Euler detonates there. One transcendental per link per tick buys that entire failure class away, which `thermalLinkAbsurdGeometry` checks over 5,000 ticks: no oscillation, no crossing, no non-finite value, and the pair still settles on the energy-weighted average with its ledger closed.

**A Thermal link is mass-free.** It modifies no gas inventory, no species and no vapour mass. The only vapour change it can cause is the one the destination's own saturation solve produces in response to the heat that arrived, which is a consequence of the heat, not a transfer of gas. This is what keeps the link from being a disguised Atmosphere pathway and is checked directly by `thermalLinkMassUntouched` over a long conduction run.

**The whole pass runs before the per-compartment loop, on the thermal state left by the reservoir exchange.** No compartment has been integrated yet when conduction is evaluated. The link pass seeds its provisional running temperatures with the already-booked reservoir exchanger transfer divided by each compartment's capacity (#833); otherwise two individually stable passive solves can spend the same starting heat and publish below absolute zero. Each link's energy transfer is then folded into its endpoints' external heat rate sum — the same contract the reservoir exchange already uses. Exchanged and conducted heat remain separately attributed, and neither provisional update is applied a second time to the compartment. This placement is not interchangeable: it is the only one where heat that has just arrived meets the destination's coupled vapour/thermal solve in the same tick, preserving the standing guarantee that a published compartment is never supersaturated against its own state. Placed after the loop the ledger would still close perfectly while a supersaturated compartment published for a tick, and conduction would be evaluated after rather than before the coupled phase solve. The consequence is that heat drawn out of a warm compartment by a cold neighbour condenses moisture out of that compartment in the same tick, so a cold wall behaves like a cold wall (`thermalLinkSameTickCondensation`), and the coupled solve itself needed no change at all. Within the pass, links are resolved sequentially pairwise in a canonical order — operator splitting, each link relaxing the running temperature its predecessors left rather than the shared tick-start value — rather than as a coupled matrix: parallel links then compose exactly additively, a second link cannot re-move heat the first already moved, the no-crossing property survives the pass regardless of order, and every joule stays attributable to one named link. The order is canonical for the wiring rather than for the order the author typed the entries, so the splitting error a reshuffled document would otherwise introduce is the same error every time and no published number can move when the document is reshuffled. An implicit sparse Laplacian would be unconditionally stable and order-independent but is a solver dependency the project has deliberately avoided and buys nothing measurable at these time constants.

**Links report as a flux, not as a service.** `StationThermalLinkSnapshot` sits beside the atmosphere and pathway snapshots carrying a signed last-step flow rate in kW, cumulative net energy transferred, and an `Idle`/`Active` status; each compartment gains its own conducted total and last-tick rate, and every published member is registered in the observable registry. There are deliberately no `requested` / `actual` / `limiter` fields. Nothing asks for heat to move: the flow is wholly determined by the temperature difference, so there is no request, no shortfall and no limiter to name. Forcing the service shape would make every link report that it received everything it asked for on every tick, which reads as a healthy service and reports nothing at all — the same reasoning that made pathways report flux.

Reversing any of these changes the model rather than its implementation. Merging links into pathways, or into Station connections, re-creates the fake-one-to-get-the-other problem and the directed double-resolution bug class. Reusing the star solve requires inventing a capacity for a connector that has none and importing reservoir semantics into a wall. Stepping the conduction explicitly reintroduces overshoot against absurd authored geometry, and clamping the overshoot substitutes bookkeeping for physics while the energy ledger still closes. Moving the pass after the per-compartment loop silently publishes supersaturated compartments for a tick. And adopting the service vocabulary would report a request that was never made. The ledger keeps the honesty visible either way: each endpoint books its own cumulative conducted energy, `a` decremented and `b` incremented by the same number as an exact negation, and the compartment energy residual carries a `+ conductedKJ` term beside the occupant, crew, crop, algae, latent and exchanged terms — miss that booking and the existing conservation checks fail loudly rather than quietly.
